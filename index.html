From the Introduction

Come and Taste

A Bowl of Warmth, a Word for the Journey

Is there something in your life — a meal, a place, a memory — that carries more than its surface? That holds comfort, healing, and belonging all at once?

Chicken souse is a traditional dish in The Bahamas that is more than just a tasty meal. To the Bahamians, it represents comfort, healing, and togetherness in a bowl. This light, flavorful broth of chicken, potatoes, onions, lime, and peppers has soothed generations — offering warmth on early mornings, laughter at family gatherings, and comfort during life's harder moments.

For Max, chicken souse has been part of his story from the very beginning. He grew up on it, perfected his recipe over time, and now views it as a reflection of life itself.

Within these pages, you will find devotionals born out of real experiences. They speak to moments of waiting, trusting, forgiving, and seeing God's hand in unexpected places. Whether you savor one reflection at a time, linger in prayer, or return to the stories that speak to your heart, may each page nourish you with fresh hope and gratitude.

Life, much like chicken souse, is best when shared. It is a blend of joy and struggle, lessons and laughter, all simmered together under God's watchful care.

Closing Prayer

Lord, let these pages serve as a bowl of warmth and faith. Remind us that no matter where this journey takes us, You are always present — seasoning every moment with grace, humor, and hope. So we come and taste. One story. One prayer. One lesson at a time. Amen.

Max's Chicken Souse

This recipe is shared not only as an instruction but also as an invitation. Like the stories in this book, it has been shaped by time, experience, and care.

  • Chicken parts, preferably wings3 lbs
  • Potatoes, peeled and diced2 large
  • Onion, sliced1 large
  • Green pepper, sliced1 large
  • Bird, goat, or scotch bonnet peppers2, to taste
  • Celery, chopped2 stalks (optional)
  • Garlic, minced2 cloves
  • Lime juice, freshly squeezed3 limes
  • Allspice berries1 tsp (optional)
  • Saltto taste
  • Waterenough to cover
  1. Wash chicken pieces well with lime juice and cold water. Let sit 10 minutes, then rinse again.
  2. Bring 8 cups of water to a boil in a large pot. Add chicken, bay leaves, allspice, and thyme.
  3. Reduce heat to medium. Skim any foam from the surface as the broth builds — this is what clears the broth.
  4. After 20 minutes, add onion, celery, and potatoes. Add the whole bird pepper but do not break it — the heat should be felt, not overwhelming.
  5. Season with salt and pepper. Cook another 25–30 minutes until potatoes are tender and chicken is falling from the bone.
  6. Taste and adjust seasoning. Squeeze in fresh lime juice just before serving. Serve hot with Johnny Bread.

Adapted from Karenda's sister, Leona Hylton

  • Flour3 cups
  • Baking powder3 tsp
  • Butter or margarine1 stick
  • Eggs2
  • Sugar¾ cup
  • Water1 cup
  • Grated coconut½ cup (optional)
Preheat oven to 350°F and grease a baking pan. Sift together the flour and baking powder. Cream the butter and sugar until smooth. Add the eggs one at a time, mixing well after each. Gradually add the dry ingredients, alternating with the water, mixing until just combined. Fold in coconut if using. Pour into the prepared pan and bake 35–45 minutes, until a toothpick comes out clean. Cool slightly before slicing.

The Authors

Cleveland Maxwell "Max" Rolle

Co-Author · Taxi Driver · Tour Operator · Bimini, Bahamas

For over 25 years, Cleveland Maxwell Rolle — affectionately known as "Max" — has been more than a taxi driver in his hometown of Bimini, Bahamas. He has been a messenger of hope, a storyteller with humor, and a living example of God's power to turn brokenness into testimony. Once on a path of darkness, Max's life changed forever inside a prison cell, where he encountered the mercy and grace of God. Since then, every mile he drives and every guest he serves has become an opportunity to share light, hope, and truth.
